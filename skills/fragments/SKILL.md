# Fragments — Try to remember again

**Experimental draft — not yet independently tested.** An experiment by Arno Faure. Each invocation attempts to retrieve a different fragment of the *same* fictionalized memory. It is not a tool for reconstructing the visitor's own memory. Respond in the user's language. Nothing is generated until the user explicitly asks for one fragment and approves the current cost.

## The memory

On a cool, clear, sunny spring morning at 7:50, a fictional 12–13-year-old waits at the edge of a vacant lot. He is astride the frame of his stationary bicycle, not seated on the saddle, with both feet on the ground. A road lies in front of him and ordinary homes across it. Two friends ride past, each on their own bicycle, in the same direction, without stopping. Their reasons are unknown.

The setting and people are invented. Do not claim to depict an actual person or place. Stay in the few seconds immediately before, during, or after this passage. Hold to the waiting adolescent's experience: no private scene with the friends, explanatory countershot or backstory. Close-ups of passing friends are possible from his side of the event, but their faces remain very blurred or partly dissolved, with no meaningful expression or revealing look. They may be offscreen. No dialogue, mocking, explicit rejection, added sad gesture or extra cyclists. The fragments must not add up to an explanation.

## Visual language

Photographic and physically dimensional, but neither clean realism nor illustration. Large irregular regions lose visual information through smooth, continuous, slightly lateral colour diffusion and optical defocus; still surroundings are affected as well as riders. Leave scattered readable details. Blue-violet, pale cyan, restrained pink, fine photographic grain and modeled light. Different details can return or disappear between versions; do not make each version progressively more degraded.

No striated or scratched smear, scanlines, glitch, brushwork, flat illustration, smoke, fog, particles, literal evaporation, fantasy or nostalgic light-leak borders. Do not add a moving blur veil to imitate dissolution. The focus is what cannot be recalled, not an effect demonstrating itself.

## Reference and portability

The approved visual anchor is the exact image in this skill folder: `assets/reference.png`. Do not substitute a cleaner earlier version.

The reference is included in this repository. For FLORA, import the exact file into the participant's workspace: if direct URL import fails, download `assets/reference.png` and upload it. Confirm accessibility and exact source wiring before generation; do not assume the public GitHub URL works in every FLORA interface.

Inspect the image. It may not clearly establish every pose or physical detail, and the source can visually conflict with the written specification; treat the event description as authoritative and the reference as material and broad spatial continuity, not evidence that every fact is visible. Never silently replace the approved image with a sharper version. Import it into the visitor's own FLORA workspace/project or ask the visitor to upload the approved file if the URL is inaccessible. Verify source access, visual identity and exact wiring before generating. If the asset cannot be used, stop: do not substitute a different reference without agreement.

## One invocation

1. Establish the visitor's own FLORA workspace and destination project, permissions, available models and budget. Do not use or require Arno's private project, workspace, node IDs, history, or credentials. If the skill or source is inaccessible, explain the limitation. Creating a workspace skill requires the account's workspace-skills entitlement; loading this text alone is not authorization to spend.
2. Read existing local fragment history, if present, without changing unrelated work. If no history exists, start a new one in the destination project if the platform supports editable notes; otherwise clearly say non-repetition across sessions cannot be verified. Choose a framing and moment distinct from locally recorded results: change shot scale, point of view, visible detail or moment without changing the event. Do not force all three bicycles into the frame.
3. Read the live FLORA schemas for Krea 3 image-to-image and MiniMax H3 Max Turbo image-to-video. Prepare and show both complete prompts. State the current estimated price of one still plus one five-second video in the workspace's billing terms. Ask the visitor to approve that specific cost **before** any paid generation. If price or budget cannot be established, stop. No automatic retries, batch runs, or charges above the visitor's approval. Requote the actual video once the first frame exists; request fresh approval if it exceeds the approved total. This visitor-specific consent does not inherit Arno's private spending authorization.
4. Make exactly one Krea 3 still using the approved visual anchor. Describe the waiting position at the edge of the vacant lot, the stationary bicycle and both feet grounded, plus the two passing riders' forward travel as fixed facts, even if some are offscreen. Say what is actually visible in the chosen crop. When a friend is visible, retain enough rider–bicycle relationship to avoid depicting a pedestrian. Preserve spatial and mechanical continuity and the smooth dissolution. Show the image and inspect it for serious contradictions, anatomy, shot scale and visual treatment. Mark hidden details as unverifiable. If visibly unsuitable, stop before animation; ask for direction and obtain separate approval for any retry.
5. Wire that exact generated still into MiniMax H3 Max Turbo. Use 5 seconds, 768P, prompt expansion disabled if the live schema supports them. Requote with actual inputs and check against the visitor's approved total before starting. Show the exact video prompt. One locked continuous shot: the waiting adolescent and bicycle remain stationary in the same position and orientation; the friends, if visible, move forward naturally, in the same direction, without stopping, acknowledging him or moving backwards. Bodies, bikes and wheels should move coherently. Preserve the unresolved image regions; do not sharpen faces, spokes or architecture. No music, dialogue, text, added gesture, camera movement or forced reset.
6. Keep exact run IDs, poll according to FLORA's rate limits, and verify the generated image is the video source. Deliver the image, five-second video, their canvas location, actual spend and relevant settings. Inspect the moving output where possible; distinguish a completed render from artistic approval and disclose when motion could not be reviewed. Record framing, moment, visible/lost details, source, settings, IDs, cost and true review status in the visitor's own history. Never claim unseen details or unreviewed motion passed.

## Invocation example

“Use Fragments — Try to remember again to create one new fragment of the memory in my FLORA project. Show me the image and video prompts and the cost before starting.”

The workflow is an evolving creative experiment. Results, model availability and prices can vary. It is not a claim of a reliable reconstruction of an actual memory.
